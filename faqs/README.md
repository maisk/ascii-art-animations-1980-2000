# The ASCII Art FAQs (1993–2007)

Five FAQs of the Usenet ASCII art newsgroups, in six files. They explain what ASCII art was, how to view and draw it, how
to post it, and how to play the animations that fill this repository. Each one is the latest version found of its
author's FAQ; Jorn Barger's is here twice, in his own layout of 1993 and in the reformatted copy that circulated later.

## What a Usenet FAQ was

Before the web, much of the Internet's conversation took place on **Usenet**, a worldwide system of discussion groups
called *newsgroups* (alt.ascii-art, rec.arts.ascii, alt.ascii-art.animation …). Messages were copied from server to
server and read with a *newsreader*. New readers kept asking the same questions, so each group had a **FAQ**, a list of
"frequently asked questions" with their answers, kept by a volunteer.

The FAQ was **posted to the group itself** at regular intervals (weekly or monthly), so that every newcomer would find it
among the latest messages. The big ones were also posted to `news.answers` and archived by `rtfm.mit.edu`. Mirror sites
such as faqs.org offered them on the web. A FAQ grew from version to version. Its header carried the version, the date of
the last change and an `Archive-name`, the FAQ's name in the archives. When a maintainer gave up, someone else picked
the FAQ up, often reusing parts of the old text.

The ASCII art FAQ is such a chain. Jorn Barger wrote the first one in 1993. Bob Allison ("Scarecrow") then kept the FAQ of
the groups until 1996, and Matthew Thomas wrote a new one in 1997–98. CJRandall, who reposted the older FAQs in 1998–99,
later merged the three into his version 3 (found from 2002 on) and kept posting it until at least 2009. Martin Atkins' mini-FAQ is a short companion about fonts, editors and tools.

The texts are plain ASCII and look right only in a **fixed-width (monospaced) font**. The pictures in them are part of
the text. Their authors meant them to be read that way: CJRandall's FAQ even says "Public display of this document in
proportional-fonts is forbidden".

The file names start with the date of the version, from the FAQ's own header. The date of the posting is in the `Date:`
line at the top of the files that came from Usenet.

What to look for, for the animations:
- **Barger:** its stated purpose is "to promote more creative use of the ASCII character set on Internet, especially
  for page layout and animation". "The faster your modem, the nicer this works."
- **Allison:** "how to put an animation in your .plan" (VT100 animations were shown with `finger`), and how to save,
  uudecode and play the animation files posted to the groups.
- **Thomas:** what belongs in the groups and what does not. ANSI ("high ASCII") art is not welcome. HTML art and
  JavaScript animations go on a web page, and only the address is posted.
- **Randall:** section [18], "What is ascii-animation?": the early portable kind used the control codes of the
  terminal (`.VT`, `.ANS` files), the later web kind Java or JavaScript.

## How the texts were treated

The texts are as posted, with these changes only:
- The HTML of the web copies was reduced to plain text. Line ends are LF.
- Only the `Subject`, `Date` and `Newsgroups` header lines of the Usenet posts were kept.
- **Email addresses were masked (`XXXXX`)**, except the authors' own addresses in their signatures and the addresses of
  services (listserv, ftpmail).

Many web addresses in the FAQs no longer work. They are left as they were.

Older versions also exist: Allison's from March 1994 on, Thomas' 1.0.2 to 2.0 (1997–98) and CJRandall's 3.0.3.12 (2002)
and 3.0.3.19 (2004). They are not included here because each is superseded by the version above.

The FAQs belong to their authors. They are included as historical documents, like the animations (see `../NOTICE.md`).
